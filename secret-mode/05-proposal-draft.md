# Portfolio & Programme — engagement proposal
### Kritiq × Secret Mode · 6 months · draft v0.1

*Prepared for Dan Robinson · sponsor: Ed Blincoe*

---

## The problem, as you described it

Secret Mode is building faster than its planning can keep up with. Two things follow from that, and you named both:

**There is no coherent plan for the catalogue.** Titles you want to do more with — Chained Together on console, Asia, others — have no value-creation plan behind them. There's no tiering, so no shared language for what a tentpole is versus what catalogue is. And that isn't a documentation gap: *you can't tier a portfolio before you know what each title could become.* The growth plans have to come first.

**And nobody holds the pen.** Commercial signs the deals and secures the platform commitments. Marketing takes titles to market. The franchise plan that should sit between them — what this IP becomes, in what order, funded how — belongs to neither, so it doesn't get written.

Underneath both: eleven launches of note currently sitting in March 2027, four live-service titles under a single owner, and a marketing group of 25–30 running on community feedback with no analyst and no telemetry.

## What this engagement is

**Someone picks up the pen, produces a v1.0 the whole business believes in, and then makes themselves unnecessary.**

Three phases, six months, 2–3 days a week.

---

## Phase 1 · Weeks 1–6 — Establish the picture

You can't plan a portfolio you can't see. This phase produces the facts that currently live in several heads and no documents.

- **Catalogue audit.** Every title: rights position (owned outright / publishing rights / rev-share), what reverts and when, what's already been granted away, contribution, current state.
- **Data and evidence audit.** What feeds exist, what's contractually available from dev partners, and what's simply unplumbed. Output is a specification, not a wish.
- **The March 2027 map.** All eleven launches on one page with their real dependencies and their real constraints, separating what's fixed from what merely feels fixed.

**Immediate output:** a de-conflicted launch calendar. This is the first thing that changes, because it's the thing hurting now.

## Phase 2 · Weeks 6–16 — Portfolio Map v1.0

The destination: **what success looks like in January 2028**, and the route to it.

- **Value-creation plan per priority IP.** What each title could become, the ranked moves, and the evidence behind each. Chained Together console and Asia are the obvious first two.
- **Tiering, derived not declared.** Once each title has a growth thesis, Tier A / B / C assigns itself — and, importantly, so does the resourcing each tier earns.
- **Sequenced against real capacity**, including Secret Code's ramp and the Lab42 handover on Chained Together.
- **The Jan 2028 picture** — what the portfolio looks like, what it earns, and what has been proved by then.

Built *with* Marketing, Commercial and Publishing as co-authors, not delivered to them. A plan the business believes in is one it helped write; anything else is a document that gets praised once and ignored thereafter.

## Phase 3 · Weeks 16–26 — Make it run without me

A map is worthless if refreshing it requires me.

- **Decision rights.** Who owns the franchise plan for each IP, who signs what, and how Marketing, Commercial, Publishing and Secret Code interlock. Written down.
- **The operating cadence.** A portfolio review rhythm that actually runs — agenda, inputs, templates, owners — so the map stays current rather than going stale in a drive.
- **The evidence layer.** Analyst role scoped and, ideally, hired; the first telemetry pipes specified and in progress.
- **The permanent pen.** Role definition for whoever owns this after me, and support recruiting them.

## What good looks like at month six

- The exec, Commercial and Publishing all reference the same portfolio map, unprompted.
- March 2027 has become a survivable shape.
- Tier assignments are agreed, and resourcing follows them.
- Two or three value-creation plans are **in execution**, not just written.
- Someone internal holds the pen, and the review rhythm runs whether or not I'm in the room.
- An analyst is in post and the first data is flowing.

## Two things worth saying now

**Hire an analyst before you spend much more on advice.** A 25–30 person marketing group with no analyst is the cheapest gap in the business to close, and it's the precondition for every recommendation I make being evidence-led rather than well-argued. I'd rather tell you that in week one than bill you for the workaround.

**Decide your rule on licensed work before Galactic Racer launches.** If it goes as well as you expect, the pull toward more top-tier licensed work will intensify — and that work buys reputation and cash flow while consuming exactly the capacity your owned IP needs to build equity value. That's not an argument against taking it. It's an argument for knowing, in advance and in writing, what you'd say yes to and what you wouldn't. Much easier to decide now than in the middle of a good quarter.
