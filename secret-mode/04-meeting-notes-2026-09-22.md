# Secret Mode — session notes, 22 Sep 2026

**Room:** Ed Blincoe (CEO) · Dan Robinson (VP Marketing) · Joe Waterworth (Publishing Director)
**Outcome:** discussed Kritiq engaging ~6 months, 2–3 days/week. Proposal to follow.

---

## What they said

**Self-diagnosis:** building quickly, and at the point where they know they will make mistakes purely from pipeline load.

**Launches**
- Star Wars: Galactic Racer, 6 Oct. Confident. Good for reputation; if it goes gangbusters it may create "what more can you do, Secret Mode?" pull for more AAA support — unknown until after launch.
- A second top-tier IP signed, unannounced. Credibility, **no shareholder value in Secret Mode**.
- **11 launches of note currently queued for March 2027** (Dan).

**Area 1 — Portfolio Map: what does success look like in Jan 2028**
- No coherent plan for the catalogue.
- Key titles they want to do more with but have no value-creation plan for — e.g. Chained Together on console, Asia.
- No tiering. Tier A vs Tier C, catalogue vs tentpole, undefined — and *because there's no growth plan, they don't know which should be which.*
- **Commercial sits separate from Marketing.** Commercial is largely BD: signing MGs, securing platform marketing commits. The portfolio / commercial / franchise plan per IP falls between the two. **No one has the pen.**

**Area 2 — Capacity / live-ops structure**
- Living week to week across numerous launches and announcements.
- **Joe owns four live-service titles alone:** Chained Together, Escape the Backrooms, SCUM, Squad (to be announced).
- Marketing ~25–30 people. **No analytics resource.** Data feeds exist but no analyst has crunched them. **No game telemetry.** Running on community feedback, social listening, Discord.
- Large launches like Star Wars pull resources off live-service titles.
- Some dev partners are indifferent about live-service future updates. Building an internal team — **Secret Code** — to take over live updates end to end. Promising, but 3–6 months to stand up and transition.

## Read

**1. The March 2027 collision sits inside the engagement.** Eleven launches of note, roughly five months out. If the engagement starts in October, the crunch lands in month five — this is not a planning exercise with a distant horizon, it's steering through a pile-up that's already visible. That's the credibility-earning moment; the Jan 2028 map is the durable output. The proposal has to do both, and should say so.

**2. "No one has the pen" is the engagement.** They diagnosed it themselves, which is the best possible starting position — nobody needs persuading a gap exists. The live question is whether Kritiq *holds* the pen for six months or *builds the function* that holds it permanently. Almost certainly both, but which is primary changes the framing, the internal title, and the exit.

**3. Tiering can't come first, and they already know it.** "Because there is no growth plan they don't know which should be which." So the order is: growth-plan hypotheses per IP → tiering falls out of that → resourcing follows the tiering. Anyone who starts by assigning Tier A/B/C is guessing.

**4. No analyst in a 25–30 person marketing team is the cheapest fix available.** It's also the precondition for everything else being evidence-led rather than vibes-led. Worth being straight that some of the value here is saying *hire this person instead of paying me for it* — that earns more trust than it costs in fees, and an analyst plus telemetry makes every subsequent recommendation defensible.

**5. Joe is a single point of failure and that's a business risk, not a workload complaint.** Four live titles, one owner. If Joe is ill, on leave, or leaves, four revenue-generating community relationships wobble at once. Worth naming to Ed in those terms.

**6. Star Wars success is a trap worth pre-committing against.** If it goes gangbusters, the pull toward more licensed AAA work intensifies — more revenue, more reputation, **zero equity** — and it consumes exactly the capacity the owned IP needs to build value. The second signed top-tier IP means this is already happening. They should decide their rule for accepting licensed work *before* the answer arrives, not during the euphoria. That's a pre-commitment only an outsider will raise.
