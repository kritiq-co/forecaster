## 3. The comparison you're actually worried about

You're right that a naive comparison looks bad, and right to want to know before you quote.

**On an annualised-rate basis, yes, it looks higher.** £1,300/day at 2.5 days a week annualises to ~£169k. That's above the estimated VP base.

**On a total-spend basis, it doesn't.** Six months at £14k/month is £84k. Six months of a £125k VP's *fully loaded* cost is roughly £76k. **You'd cost about the same as Dan for the same period, for a specialist capability they currently cannot buy at all.**

Both statements are true. Which one Dan reaches for depends entirely on how you present the number — which is the actual lever here.

**And what they're not paying for:** employer NI (~£18k/yr on a £125k salary), pension, holiday, sick pay, benefits, equipment, notice period, redundancy liability, or the utilisation risk between engagements — all of which sit with you.

**Nor the cost of the alternative.** A permanent hire into this gap means a retained search at 20–35% of first-year comp — **£25–44k in fees alone** — plus three to six months to fill, plus onboarding. March 2027 arrives before that person does.

## 4. Where your rate should sit

The band is £800–£1,800/day for fractional, with interim at a 20–40% premium.

**You're closer to interim than fractional.** Fractional is advisory — a hand on the tiller. You'd be holding the pen, owning an output, and named as accountable for it. That's the interim end, which supports the upper half of the band.

Pulling the other way: it's early for Kritiq, you want the relationship, and six months at 2–3 days is real committed volume, which normally earns something.

## 5. The tactical point — and you already solved it

**Never quote a day rate. Quote the total and the monthly.**

Your Curse Forge proposal does exactly this: *"£xxxx over the 16-week period, invoiced monthly (£xk)."* No day rate anywhere. Keep that.

It matters because of how people compare. A day rate gets compared to a payslip — Dan will instinctively divide his own salary by 228 and feel the gap. A monthly retainer gets compared to budget lines: agency fees, tooling, contractor spend. Against a 25–30 person marketing function and an agency roster, £14k a month is an unremarkable line item. Against Dan's daily rate of pay, £1,290 is a conversation.

Same money. Completely different question in the reader's head.

**If Dan asks for the day rate, answer in days, not rate:** *"Two to three days a week, £14k a month."* That's a complete answer and it doesn't reopen the comparison.

## 6. Practicalities

- **VAT.** Quote as "+ VAT" or "plus VAT if applicable". Secret Mode reclaims it, so it's neutral to them, but silence now becomes an awkward 20% conversation at invoice one. This engagement alone is close to the UK registration threshold (£90k) — worth a word with your accountant before you quote.
- **Consistency with Curse Forge.** Whatever day rate your Curse Forge number implies is an anchor. These are both UK games companies with overlapping networks and people talk. If Secret Mode's implied rate is materially higher, have a reason ready — scope, accountability, interim vs advisory — or align them.
- **Expenses.** Travel to site, if they want you in person. Either "plus reasonable pre-agreed expenses" or fold it in and say it's inclusive. Don't leave it unsaid.
- **Ask which budget line it comes from.** A genuinely useful question, and cheap to ask. If it's agency or consultancy budget, £14k/month is small and you may be under-asking. If it's headcount budget, the comparison to Dan becomes live and the monthly framing matters even more.
- **Month-three review is your uplift mechanism.** If March needs four days a week — and you've said it will — that's the moment to reprice, from a position of having delivered. Better than padding the initial number and risking the yes.
