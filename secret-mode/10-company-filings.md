## Two things that matter

**1. The numbers you want are probably not filed.** Secret Mode Limited's accounts category is
**"Audit Exemption Subsidiary"** — small-company filleted accounts. Those carry a balance sheet
and notes but typically **no profit and loss account**, so turnover and profit are likely not
public at all. A directory banding of "turnover under £15M, fewer than 50 employees" is an
estimate, not filed data, and it predates the current headcount.

What you realistically can pull, free, from the filed accounts:

- Net assets and shareholders' funds
- Fixed and current assets, creditors, cash
- **Average employee number** — usually in the notes
- **Staff costs**, where disclosed
- Directors' remuneration, sometimes, in the holdco

Average headcount plus staff costs gives you **cost per head**, which is the number that grounds
the Dan comparison in `08-pricing.md`. That is worth five minutes.

**2. Both filings are due on 30 September 2026.** Eight days away, and they matter:

- It is the **first full year under Emona** and the **first accounts the holdco has ever filed**.
- If you start on 12 October, the first post-buyout picture is public in week one.
- It is a natural, non-intrusive thing to raise with Ed: *"your FY25 accounts are going in this
  month — what do they say, and is that the base we plan against?"* That is a legitimate
  question from the person holding the pen, and it opens the financial conversation without
  you having to ask for the P&L cold.

## Set expectations, though

**Statutory filings will not answer the portfolio question.** Small-company accounts show a
balance sheet for a group, not contribution per title. Nothing at Companies House will tell you
what Chained Together earns versus Backrooms, which is exactly what the growth plans need.

That is what the catalogue audit in Phase 1 is for, and it needs management accounts, not the
public record. Worth knowing before anyone assumes the register will do the work — and worth
saying to Dan, because "we'll pull the accounts" is a common and comfortable substitute for
asking finance the harder question.
