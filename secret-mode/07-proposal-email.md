Hi Dan,

Thanks for the time yesterday — genuinely useful session, and good to meet Joe properly. As promised, here's my understanding of where things stand and what I'd propose. Correct anything I've got wrong before we go further.

**Where Secret Mode is**

You're growing quickly and the portfolio has grown with you. Galactic Racer lands in a fortnight, a second top-tier IP is signed, Secret Code is standing up, and caretaker development has given you four live-service titles where eighteen months ago you had a slate of premium launches. That's a good problem, but it's outrun the planning around it — and you were all clear-eyed that mistakes are coming purely from load rather than from judgement.

The goal, as I understood it, is to know what success looks like by January 2028 and to have a plan behind each title that the whole business believes in.

**Areas that need immediate support**

*Portfolio plan.* There's no coherent view across the catalogue, and several titles you want to do more with — Chained Together on console, Asia — have no value-creation plan behind them. Tiering is the obvious first instinct, but it can't come first: until each title has a growth thesis, you can't say which should be a tentpole and which is catalogue. The growth plans come first and the tiering falls out of them.

*Programme management.* Eleven launches of note currently sit in March 2027, and the team is living week to week. What's missing is a holistic view of the whole slate — real dependencies, real constraints, and an honest separation of what's genuinely fixed from what only feels fixed. This is the thing hurting now, so it's where I'd start.

*Ownership.* Commercial signs the deals and secures the platform commitments. Marketing takes titles to market. The franchise plan that sits between them belongs to neither, so it doesn't get written. That's the gap I'd be filling — and the job is to fill it, build a v1.0 everyone believes in, then hand the pen to someone internal and make myself unnecessary.

**Outline of engagement**

- Two to three days a week across all three areas, including a weekly sync.
- Starting 12 October. Initial term six months, two months' notice either side.
- £72,000 plus VAT over the 26-week period, invoiced monthly (£12k).
- To be reviewed in early January to agree what the second half looks like.

One ask: I'd like a short conversation with Alison before we finalise this. The franchise plan is as much Commercial's as Marketing's, and it'll be a better piece of work — and a far more likely one to stick — if she's shaped the scope rather than received it.

Two honest notes to finish. First, I think an analyst is the cheapest gap you have to close, and I'd rather say that now than quietly work around it for six months — with no telemetry and nobody crunching the feeds you already have, everything any of us recommends is an argument rather than evidence. Second, my gut says March will need more than three days a week, but I'd rather start here and flex than pad it up front. I'm here to help.
